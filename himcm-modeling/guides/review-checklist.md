# Review checklist

Use this on a full draft. It is a list of questions, not a template: a paper can answer them in many ways. Items marked ★ are the ones most worth the time when time is short.

## Rules and completeness

- [ ] ★ Every requirement and sub-requirement in the current statement is answered, and a reader can find each answer quickly (heading tags, a mapping table, or a restatement the paper follows).
- [ ] ★ The paper meets the current page limit, counting everything the statement says counts.
- [ ] All required parts are present in the required form: summary sheet, table of contents, references, and the memo, letter, article or app page if asked.
- [ ] The paper is identified as the current contest instructions require (normally the team control number only).
- [ ] Every requested value (a forecast year, a schedule, a choice, bounds) is stated as a number or a table, not left to be read off a plot.

## Summary sheet

- [ ] ★ It stands alone: the problem, the approach, the main results with numbers where relevant, and the recommendation.
- [ ] Each paragraph leads with a finding or key idea, not only a list of methods.
- [ ] Every number matches the body exactly.
- [ ] It mentions how the results were checked (validation or sensitivity) in a phrase.

## Problem reading and modeling

- [ ] ★ The key term ("best", "impact", "drought", ...) is defined in one clear sentence early on and used consistently.
- [ ] Each assumption has a justification and is actually used. The assumptions that drive the results are tested.
- [ ] Each method choice has a reason linked to the question or the data.
- [ ] Every constant, weight and threshold is sourced, calibrated or explained, and the important ones are varied.
- [ ] Missing data, outliers and imputation are reported with a check.
- [ ] Generated, assumed or illustrative data are labeled as such.
- [ ] Parts of the paper connect: outputs of one part feed the next where the problem implies it (for example, a forecast sizing a plan).
- [ ] ★ At least one worked example traces a real case through the model with numbers.

## Results, validation, sensitivity

- [ ] ★ Results are interpreted (why the top option is on top, why a trend appears), not only displayed.
- [ ] ★ Some form of validation suits the model: hold-out error, comparison with an independent figure, physical limits, baseline comparison, plausibility checks, convergence.
- [ ] ★ Sensitivity analysis varies the inputs that matter and says what it means for the conclusion.
- [ ] Long extrapolations state their training window, are compared with physical limits, and are presented as scenarios where appropriate.
- [ ] Uncertainty is shown where a requirement asks for bounds or reliability (intervals, ranges, scenarios).
- [ ] Strengths and weaknesses are specific to this model.

## Consistency and accuracy

- [ ] ★ Text, equations, tables, figures and code agree (weights, signs, parameter values, the optimization direction).
- [ ] Headline numbers recomputed by hand once; units checked in every formula (m³ vs L, t vs Mt, % vs share).
- [ ] Each quantity has one value throughout: summary, body, conclusion, memo or article.
- [ ] Each thing has one name throughout.

## Presentation

- [ ] A workflow figure or roadmap shows the overall approach.
- [ ] Every figure and table is referenced in the text, has a caption that says what to notice, and has labeled axes with units.
- [ ] Displayed equations are numbered and each is explained in words.
- [ ] Notation is defined in a table and at first use.
- [ ] Sources are cited for all data, formulas and facts, in a consistent style.

## Non-technical piece

- [ ] ★ Written for the named reader, in their language, with no technical terms.
- [ ] Gives one to three memorable numbers that match the paper, and a clear recommendation.
- [ ] Looks like the genre requested (memo header, letter salutation and signature, article headline and image, app screens).
- [ ] States the answer the reader asked for in the opening paragraph, and explains how to read any table or schedule it includes.
- [ ] Fits its length limit.

## Final pass

- [ ] Spelling and grammar checked; long sentences split.
- [ ] Page breaks, figure placement and fonts checked in the final PDF.
- [ ] Read once from start to finish as a judge would: summary, contents, headings, figures, conclusion. Is the story clear from those alone?
