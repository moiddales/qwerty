Summary
To ensure the modernity and global appeal of Olympic events, thereby catering to the demands of
contemporary audiences and achieving broader cultural inclusivity [1], a comprehensive evaluation
and event selection model has been established based on the standards of the International Olympic
Committee (IOC). This model takes into account factors such as popularity, gender equality,
sustainability, safety and fair competition, as well as economic benefits, aiming to provide a
scientific basis for the IOC’s decision-making regarding new sports, disciplines, and events (SDEs).
Task 1. Based on the standards of the IOC, we have selected seven evaluation indicators:
popularity and accessibility, gender equality, sustainability, inclusivity, relevance and innova-
tion, safety and fair competition, as well as economic benefits and commercialization potential.
Quantitative calculation expressions have been established for each indicator, and the values of these
seven indicators for 48 Olympic events, including aquatic sports, archery, athletics, ice hockey, etc.,
have been calculated. The detailed results are shown in Table 3.
Task 2. We established a Combined Weighting Method (CWM) and MC-TOPSIS (Monte
Carlo Simulation TOPSIS) Model to determine the SDEs that best meet the requirements of the
IOC. Firstly, the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), Entropy Weight Method, and Coefficient of
Variation Method were combined to calculate the weight matrix. Then, the CRITIC method was
used to determine the final indicator weight coefficients, reflecting the degree of influence of different
factors on the selection of SDEs. Based on this, the score rankings of the 48 events were determined,
with the top three being Athletics, Football, and Basketball. Subsequently, through comparison
using MC-TOPSIS (Monte Carlo Simulation TOPSIS), which provides multiple simulations to
ensure the robustness and anti-volatility of the scores, the SDEs that best meet the requirements of
the IOC were identified. The top three then changed to Football, Athletics, and Basketball.
Task 3. We selected three SDEs (new or removed from the Olympics): Karate, Sport Climbing,
and Offshore Rowing. Additionally, we chose six events from the existing major categories: Track
and Field, Shooting, Basketball, Gymnastics, Boxing, and Aquatic Sports. According to the model’s
calculations, the scores were as follows: Karate scored 22.674, Sport Climbing scored 33.268,
and Offshore Rowing scored 9.4959. The scores revealed varying performances among these
three newly added events. The remaining six events are traditional Olympic sports with
a stable audience base and global participation. By evaluating these sports, we verified the
efficiency and applicability of the model across different events.
Task 4. Using the simulation model we developed, we recommend Cricket, Martial Arts,
and Squash as priority new events for the 2032 Olympics. Data indicates that Cricket boasts a
significant global audience base and economic benefits, Martial Arts performs exceptionally well
in terms of cultural diversity and gender equality, and Squash aligns with sustainable development
principles. Additionally, we have identified potential events for 2036 and beyond: Esports and
Ultimate Frisbee, to cater to the interests of the new generation of audiences.
Task 5. We employed multiple weighting methods and Monte Carlo simulations to ensure
the robustness of our model. This model provides multidimensional support for the selection of
SDEs and offers a scientific basis for the IOC to make balanced decisions between tradition and
innovation.
Finally, we provided the IOC with scientific decision-making evidence and conclusions for the
selection of SDEs.

Keywords: Combine Weighting Method, MC-TOPSIS, IOC, SDEs, Evaluation Factors

1 Introduction
1.1 Background
The International Olympic Committee (IOC) is preparing for the 2032 Summer Olympics in Brisbane, with
plans to adjust the Olympic events in accordance with contemporary trends in global sports and the demands
of the modern era. Throughout the history of the Olympic Games, there have been instances where certain
sports and events (SDEs) have been introduced, removed, or even reintroduced into the Olympic program.
In order to guarantee the continued relevance and appeal of the Olympic Games, it is essential that the
International Olympic Committee (IOC) conducts a thorough assessment of the sports and events (SDEs)
that should be included or removed from the programme. However, in the context of the real world, a
multitude of factors influence the potential selection of an event by the International Olympic Committee
for inclusion in the Olympic Games. Such factors include, but are not limited to, the number of viewers,
the gender ratio, the discrepancy in prize money, the number of participating countries, and the number of
doping tests.

1.2 Problem Restatment
The object for the problem is building one or more mathematical models to help the IOC evaluate which sports
are best suited for the 2032 Olympics development vision, providing recommendations for adding or removing
SDEs, and testing the modelÕs robustness. The evaluation criteria in problems includes popularity, gender
equality, sustainability, accessibility, innovation, safety and fair competition. All criteria can be quantitative
or qualitative, constant or variable, deterministic or probabilistic. At the same time, the problem also asked
to do following task:

1. Select at least three new or removed sports from the 2020, 2024, and 2028 Olympic Games for
evaluation.

2. Select at least three projects for evaluation that have been in continuous operation since 1988 or later.

3. Provide priority recommendations for adding or returning projects in 2032.

4. Assess whether there are SDEs with the potential to be included in the 2036 Olympics or later.

5. Assess the robustness of the model and analyze key factors in the model and their impact on recom-
mended results.

Besides, a one-to-two-page summary letter is required for describing the theoretical rationale for the model,
demonstrating the model’s support for the results of the SDEs assessment, and including a robustness analysis.

5  Buiding Model by Using Combine Weighting Method
To help the IOC provide a reliable, multi-dimensional evaluation model to help select the best sustainable
development initiatives that meet Olympic standards, the modeling will use the Combined Weighting Method
(CWM) to determine the weight of each evaluation indicator [4]. Specifically, we will obtain the initial
weights through the analytic hierarchy process (AHP), entropy weight method and coefficient of variation
method, and construct the weight matrix. Then, calculate the Kendall correlation coefficient and perform a
consistency test, and then use the CRITIC method to weight the weight matrix to ensure that each weight
can reflect both subjective judgment and objective differences. At the same time, the MC-TOPSIS (Monte
Carlo Simulation-TOPSIS) model is used for comparison. The final weight of each SDE is applied to Monte
Carlo simulation, and multiple scenarios are generated through a large number of random samples to examine
the ranking stability of SDE under different uncertainty conditions. This process enables identification of
the best performing SDEs under various possible scenarios and assessment of their performance differences
across weight fluctuations. The flow chart for this model is drawn in Figure 3.
