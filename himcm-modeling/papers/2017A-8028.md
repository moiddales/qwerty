# 2017A-8028: Polar Grouping and Collision-Aware Drone Animation

## At a glance
The team models launch, translation, rotation and transitions between dragon, Ferris wheel and Shenzhen Citizen Center displays. Polar-angle sorting followed by within-group radial matching reduces long transitions; multilayer and time-delay schemes avoid collisions. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They simplify drone flight to a sequence of positions and focus on image geometry and path planning. The paper builds reusable primitive actions, then composes them into a show and evaluates watching area and launch space. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Translations and arbitrary-axis rotations use matrices. Transition matching sorts drones by polar angle, partitions into groups, then matches by radius. Collision avoidance either separates altitude layers or delays one drone. Image contours and coordinates are tabulated (pp. 6–21).

## Data
The paper uses display geometry and synthetic drone coordinates. The figures are team-designed point sets or traced display contours, supplemented by drone spacing, battery, safety, FAA and cost references. Coordinates and sampling resolution are generated within the model. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes a drone can hold a prescribed 3-D coordinate and follow a planned path, with a fixed safety separation and battery duration. Display point spacing, altitude, audience buffer and launch packing are explicit design choices; regulatory constraints are discussed as requirements to verify (pp. 4–10). Its central choice is 3-D coordinates, supported by polar-angle grouping; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Tests vary group size and compare total/max travel. Launch formation, image transformations and collision schemes are demonstrated in emulation (pp. 22–31).

## What makes it stand out
The grouped polar heuristic is simple enough for a high-school implementation yet directly targets maximum travel and collision risk. Primitive-action composition makes an extensible show design.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
The contents page maps each primitive; diagrams and emulation screenshots show transformations. A memo-style conclusion reports launch and viewing requirements (pp. 3–5, 22–31).

## Notes for reuse
- The heuristic is not globally optimal and collision tests are small. An imitator should use continuous-time separation, wind bounds and a reproducible objective for group-size choice.
