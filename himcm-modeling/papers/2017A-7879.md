# 2017A-7879: Bipartite-Matched Drone Light Show

## At a glance
The team traces Ferris wheel, dragon and double-headed eagle contours in MATLAB, assigns 480 drones, and minimizes transition time by bipartite matching plus binary search on the maximum flight distance. Rotations and shears animate the figures; vertical launch/landing positions reduce staging time. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They treat the show as a sequence of point clouds. Each display is a set of target coordinates; transitions are a bottleneck, so matching is optimized before animation and launch logistics. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Image contours are sampled into drone points. Bipartite matching pairs old and new positions; binary search tests whether all pairs fit within a distance bound. Rotation/shear matrices define animation; ground points project below first-frame positions (pp. 6–24).

## Data
The paper uses display sketches, Intel safety references and FAA guidance. The figures are team-designed point sets or traced display contours, supplemented by drone spacing, battery, safety, FAA and cost references. Coordinates and sampling resolution are generated within the model. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes a drone can hold a prescribed 3-D coordinate and follow a planned path, with a fixed safety separation and battery duration. Display point spacing, altitude, audience buffer and launch packing are explicit design choices; regulatory constraints are discussed as requirements to verify (pp. 4–10). Its central choice is contour point extraction, supported by bipartite matching; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
The paper verifies matching with distance plots, timing calculations, and collision/safety checks. It reports 120–125 m height, 430×330 m restricted ground area, 480 drones and a roughly 329-second show (pp. 22–30).

## What makes it stand out
The transition objective is precise and computationally appropriate. Turning each requirement into coordinates makes the result executable, while safety and FAA constraints remain visible.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A mayoral memo states drone count, airspace and duration before mathematical details. MATLAB figures, flowcharts and a schedule make the geometric construction concrete (pp. 1–4, 17–31).

## Notes for reuse
- A 125 m altitude may require a waiver and cost data are incomplete. An imitator should include wind, communication failure and inter-drone separation in the feasibility test.
