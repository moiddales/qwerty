# 2018A-8992: Personalizing Rollercoasters: Mathematical Models for the Ranking of Rollercoasters Worldwide

## At a glance
The team designed for two users from the start: a casual explorer who wants one objective list and an enthusiast who wants a personalized one (pp. 1, 5–6). They filled missing data with "Linear Iterative Imputation," a chain of significance-pruned linear regressions applied in an order that guarantees each model's inputs exist (pp. 7–9), and then reused the structure revealed by those regressions to group variables into Thrill, Fundamentals and Inversion (pp. 11–12). The General Rollercoaster Ranking (GRR) is a linear score on standardized variables with group weights and a steel-vs-wood correction (pp. 12–14); it ranks Kingda Ka, Formula Rossa, Top Thrill Dragster and Red Force first (p. 15). The Personalized Rollercoaster Ranking (PRR) asks only three AHP questions, which is possible because of the grouping (pp. 22–25). This paper received the NCTM award.

## How they read the problem
- The introduction frames the core tension directly: an objective rating is wanted, yet "the fundamental purpose of rating rollercoasters is so that we could select ones we would enjoy" (p. 5). They resolve it with two levels of preference, the general population and the specific individual, and build one model per level on a shared base (pp. 5–6).
- They decide what "agreeing with people" should mean for the general model: it should "correspond on the universally acclaimed rollercoasters" (p. 5), which makes the comparison with online lists a validation step rather than an afterthought.
- Data decisions are argued case by case: Status is dropped as uninformative; the Harbin Happy Angel Coaster is removed because it is new, missing most statistics, and described as "a new concept" (p. 6); year of opening and ride type are left out of the general model with reasons (p. 12).

## Models and methods
- **Linear Iterative Imputation** (pp. 7–9). For each variable with missing values, regress it on the fully available candidates, drop insignificant terms at the 5% level, refit until all remain significant (worked example for Drop, Tables 2–4: Construction, Height, Speed → Height, Speed). Final models with R² (Table 5, p. 9): Speed ~ Height (0.696); Length ~ Duration, Speed (0.526); Drop ~ Speed, Height (0.954); Duration ~ Length, Construction (0.514); G Force ~ Length, Construction (0.477); Vertical Angle ~ G Force (0.233). The circular Length/Duration dependence is resolved by noticing that all four coasters missing length have duration, which gives the order Speed → Length → Drop → Duration → G Force → Vertical Angle (p. 9). Rationale: mean imputation explains none of the variance (R² of 0, p. 1).
- **Grouping from imputation structure** (pp. 11–12): variables that predict each other form Thrill (speed, drop, height) and Fundamentals (vertical angle, G-force, length, duration); inversions stand alone. "Note that we did not artificially create this grouping – it fell out naturally from the imputation process" (p. 11).
- **GRR** (pp. 12–14): r_G = β_t(thrill z-scores) + β_f(fundamentals z-scores) + β_I(inversions z-score). Group importance 5 : 5 : 1 ("fundamentals are just as important as the thrill, which is more important than the number of inversions (as inversions are usually only pursued by enthusiasts)") is divided by group size to give β_t = 5/3, β_f = 5/4, β_I = 1 (p. 13). Construction correction: the regression coefficients show wood lowers duration by 26.2 s and G-force by 0.7620, so steel coasters' fundamentals are adjusted by those amounts in standardized units "to compare Steel and Wood rides fairly" (p. 14).
- **PRR** (pp. 22–25): the user rates three pairs on Saaty's fundamental scale; the principal eigenvector of the 3×3 matrix gives group weights, again divided by group size. Worked example: a thrill-seeker answering (9, 1, 9) gives v = (9, 1, 1)/√83 and a Top 20 with seven new entries, with Top Thrill Dragster overtaking Formula Rossa (pp. 24–26).

## Data
COMAP data only for modelling (299 coasters after one removal); missing counts by column in Table 1 (p. 6), e.g., G-force 216 and vertical angle 208 missing. Means and variances used for standardization are tabulated (Table 6, p. 13). Imputation quality is checked by comparing correlation heatmaps before (complete cases) and after imputation (Figs. 1–2, pp. 10–11). Code is in R (pp. 33–38).

## Assumptions
Assumptions are placed inside each model section with justifications that admit their limits (pp. 7, 15): linear relations for imputation ("It is hard to justify that the true underlying relationships between these variables are actually linear, but… a linear model is suffice as we show through our high accuracy"); a representative dataset; a rating that is linear in each variable; and equal coefficients within each group, justified by the strong within-group correlation.

## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
- **Imputation:** R² per model and preserved correlation structure (pp. 9–11).
- **External comparison:** Coasterbuzz (user-average, mainly North American readers) and Lifed (US-based expert team); overlap counts interpreted with the readership bias in mind, and their list noted as more global (8/20 outside the USA/Canada) (pp. 16–18).
- **Sensitivity:** β_t, β_f, β_I each increased by 0.1, with full Top 20 tables, changed positions italicized and new entrants flagged (Tables 9–11, pp. 18–20). The Top 4 never moves.
- **Self-critique:** Strengths and Weaknesses for each model, including that a linear G-force term should probably taper (a logistic form is suggested) and that Kingda Ka's dominance reflects reliance on physical attributes alone (p. 21).

## What makes it stand out
- **User segmentation drives the whole design** (pp. 5–6, 28): two user types → two models on one base → two app paths. The structure is easy to follow and directly answers Tasks 1 and 3.
- **Statistical imputation with visible model selection** (pp. 7–9): backward elimination with p-values, R² reporting and a dependency-aware ordering make the data step transparent and reproducible.
- **One analysis reused three times** (pp. 11–12, 22): the imputation regressions justify the grouping, the grouping cuts personalization from 28 pairwise questions to 3, and that keeps the app simple. This is a strong example of a modelling choice justified by usability.
- **Fair cross-construction comparison** (p. 14): using fitted regression effects to adjust for wood vs steel is a principled alternative to a categorical bonus.
- **Clear, honest sensitivity presentation** (pp. 18–20): full lists with changes highlighted let readers see stability for themselves.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
- **Summary sheet:** goal and two user types → imputation method with its R² advantage → variable grouping and why → GRR → PRR → app and what it achieves ("in one click, or alternatively… with just 3 simple questions") (p. 1).
- **Paper structure:** Title page → Contents → Introduction → Data and Imputation (variables, assumptions, method, worked example, results) → GRR (approach, variables, assumptions, results and comparison, sensitivity, strengths/weaknesses) → PRR (same pattern) → News Release → App Design → References → R code.
- **Devices:** each model section repeats the same subsection pattern (approach, variables, assumptions, results, sensitivity, strengths/weaknesses); a worked example shown in full once "in the interest of clarity" (p. 8); before/after correlation plots; app flowchart (p. 28) and simple mock-ups.
- **News release:** short and conversational, opening with reader questions ("Tired of deciding which rollercoaster is the best?") and explaining objectivity in everyday terms ("how long the ride is, how much force you would experience on the ride") (p. 27).
- **Phrasing:** "Conscious that our algorithm is user-facing, it is infeasible to ask the user 28 questions just to get a personalized rollercoaster ranking." (p. 22); "This of course begs the question, which is the best?" (p. 5).

## Notes for reuse
- The 5 : 5 : 1 group weights that define the published ranking are asserted as the general population's preference, not measured (p. 13). They are tested only with +0.1 shifts, which are small relative to weights of 1–1.67.
- Two of the four Fundamentals variables are mostly imputed (G-force 216 and vertical angle 208 of 299 missing), with R² of 0.477 and 0.233, and vertical angle is predicted from already-imputed G-force (pp. 6, 9). Because Fundamentals carries the same group weight as Thrill, a large part of each score rests on imputed values.
