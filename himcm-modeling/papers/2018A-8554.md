# 2018A-8554: Get Ready for Scream

## At a glance
The team built a two-layer objective index system: the raw specifications are grouped into five rider-experience indices (ups-and-downs, vertigo, sustainability, stimulation, nostalgia), each weighted internally by the entropy weight method, and the five indices are combined either by entropy weights (for the objective "Top 10") or by user preference coefficients (for the app) (pp. 9–12). Before ranking, they corrected mislabeled type entries and filled missing values with a nearest-neighbour donor rule and within-cluster donors from hierarchical clustering run separately for wood, steel-with-inversions and steel-without-inversions coasters (pp. 5–8). Their Top 10 is led by Steel Dragon 2000, Smiler and Kingda Ka (p. 12); a recomputation on the top 200 coasters only reproduces nine of the ten (p. 14). The app, Rank&Roll, maps the five indices onto three "feelings" (excitement, intensity, vertigo) plus filter options (pp. 14–18).

## How they read the problem
- They framed the task as replacing vote-based rankings with "a series of algorithms" on objective data, then using the same structure for a personalized app (p. 4).
- The key reframing is physiological: coasters *without* inversions thrill through alternating overweight/weightlessness, coasters *with* inversions through rotation-induced vertigo (p. 8). This split drives both the imputation (steel coasters clustered separately by inversion status, p. 7) and the index design (ups-and-downs vs vertigo index, pp. 9–10).
- Wood vs steel is treated as a substantive difference, argued from the data: only 5 of 50 wooden coasters have inversions (p. 6), and steel non-inverting coasters are on average higher and faster (Table 3, p. 9).
- Safety, ticket price and weather are explicitly set aside by assumption (pp. 4–5); these are later reused to explain disagreements with online lists (p. 13).

## Models and methods
- **Error correction.** Three coasters had "Wood"/"Steel" in the Type column; after checking online they were reset to "Sit down" (Table 2, p. 5).
- **Nearest-neighbour donor imputation** for the few missing speed and length values: the donor is the coaster of the same type and construction that minimizes the sum of squared relative differences in height and length (or height and speed) (Eqs. 1–2, pp. 5–6). Rationale: speed and length are rarely missing.
- **Hierarchical clustering imputation** for drop, duration and vertical angle: wooden coasters clustered on height, speed, length (dendrogram, Fig. 1, p. 7); steel coasters split into with-inversion (clustered on height, speed, length, number of inversions) and without-inversion groups (Figs. 2–3, p. 8). A missing value is copied (or averaged) from the most similar coaster in the same cluster (p. 7). For wooden G-force (only 7 of 50 known) they reason that G-force without an acceleration system stays under 2.5G and set missing values to 2G (p. 7). The conclusion reports a cophenetic coefficient close to 1 (p. 19).
- **Quantification.** Inversions yes/no → 1/0; steel = 1 and wood = 0.828, derived from the average relative height and speed advantage of non-inverting steel coasters over wooden ones (Eq. 3, p. 9); type ranked 1–6 from sit down to inverted by "sensation intensity" (Table 4, p. 9).
- **Entropy weight method, layer 1.** Ups-and-downs index from speed, drop, G-force, vertical angle with weights 0.1864, 0.5489, 0.1967, 0.0680 (Eq. 10, p. 10); sustainability index from height, length, duration with weights 0.0057, 0.0032, 0.9911 (Eq. 12, p. 11); stimulation index from construction and type (p. 11). Vertigo index = min-max normalized number of inversions (Eq. 11, p. 10); nostalgia index = normalized age (Eq. 14, p. 11), described as "a very personalized index."
- **Layer 2.** Composite f_i = a1·s_i + a2·n_i + a3·h_i + a4·r_i + a5·Y_i with a_j ≥ 0 summing to 1 (Eq. 15, p. 11). For the objective ranking the a_j come from entropy weighting of the five indices; for the app they come from user answers (pp. 12, 14–15). Rationale for entropy weighting: it is "an objective weighting method" (p. 9).

## Data
Only the COMAP spreadsheet is used for the model; the internet is used to fix the three type errors, and the team notes that "since it is impossible to verify all the data" the given values are otherwise kept (p. 5). Missing-value counts are reported by construction group (e.g., wooden: 18 missing drop, 14 duration, 28 vertical angle, p. 7). Indices are min-max normalized before combination (Eq. 16, p. 12).

## Assumptions
Four numbered assumptions, each followed by a "Justification" paragraph (pp. 4–5): no accidents (with a cited accident probability of about one in 250 million and the absence of safety data); ticket price ignored; weather ignored because of data-collection limits; and all of height, speed, length, inversions, duration, G-force and vertical angle increase excitement "without causing any unpleasantness," which justifies treating every variable as positively oriented in the entropy method. The last assumption is revisited honestly in the conclusion ("some people will have a comfortlessness under high g force condition," p. 19).

## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
- **Comparison with online rankings** (Ranker, The Top Tens): overlap counting and relative order of shared coasters; the three coasters on both online lists (Millennium Force, Top Thrill Dragster, Kingda Ka) all appear in their Top 10, taken as "a further confirmation" of objectivity (p. 13). Differences are attributed to seat position and ticket price, both excluded by assumption (p. 13).
- **Subsample robustness**: removing the 100 lowest-ranked coasters and recomputing (entropy weights depend on the sample) leaves the top nine in membership and order except a Smiler/Kingda Ka swap; Coaster Through the Clouds replaces Steel Vengeance at 10th (Table 7, p. 14). This is a check specific to entropy weighting, whose weights shift with the sample.

## What makes it stand out
- **Mechanism-based grouping of variables** (p. 8): the acceleration-vs-rotation distinction gives each sub-index a physical story, which makes the two-layer hierarchy easy to explain and reuse in the app.
- **Group-aware imputation** (pp. 6–8): clustering within construction and inversion groups before borrowing values respects structural differences instead of using a global mean.
- **Data-derived quantification of construction** (Eq. 3, p. 9): wood's 0.828 score comes from a stated calculation rather than an arbitrary label.
- **Robustness test matched to the method** (p. 14): because entropy weights depend on the sample, re-running on a reduced sample is a sensible stability check.
- **One model, two uses** (pp. 12, 14–15): the same composite serves both the objective ranking (entropy weights) and personalization (user coefficients), so the app follows directly from Task 1.
- **App justified against explicit usability criteria** (learnability, efficiency, memorability, satisfaction, pp. 16–17), with mock-ups and three worked preference scenarios (Figs. 6–9, pp. 17–18).

## Writing and presentation worth learning
- **Summary sheet:** four paragraphs: motivation → data cleaning (correct, cluster, fill) → assumptions, method and named Top 10 with robustness result → app → three advantages and two disadvantages (p. 1). Results are named rather than quantified.
- **Paper structure:** Introduction → Assumptions and Justifications → Index notation → Data Analysis and Processing → Entropy evaluation (five indices, composite, ranking, comparison) → Sensitivity → App design → Strengths/Weaknesses → Conclusion → References → full 300-coaster ranking appendix.
- **Devices:** assumption–justification pairs; numbered sub-index sections, each ending with the fitted weight equation and a one-line reading ("drop feature plays the most important role," p. 10); dendrograms; a three-column comparison table (Online1 / Online2 / Ours, Table 6, p. 12); side-by-side robustness table (Table 7, p. 14); a tree diagram from five indices to three feelings (Fig. 4, p. 15).
- **News release:** written for fans, opening with rhetorical questions ("Have you been feeling the blood pumping in your heart…", p. 2), naming the app, and presenting the Top 10 as a simple two-column table with scores to two decimals.
- **Phrasing:** "Roller coaster without inversions tend to use the alternating transformation of overweight and weightlessness caused by rapid changes of acceleration as the main stimulation." (p. 8); "Then comes the question: How can we explain the difference between the votes and our list?" (p. 13) — a useful signpost before discussing disagreements.

## Notes for reuse
- Entropy weighting put 0.9911 of the sustainability weight on duration and 0.5489 of the ups-and-downs weight on drop (pp. 10–11), largely because the method rewards dispersion, and many of those values were imputed. Someone imitating this should check whether an "index" has collapsed into a single (partly imputed) variable before interpreting it.
- Missing wooden-coaster G-forces were set to a constant 2G (p. 7). The final composite scores in Table 5 and the appendix go above 1 and below 0 (pp. 12, 21–25), even though the indices are described as normalized, so the last aggregation step is not fully specified.
