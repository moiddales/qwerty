# 2017B-8124: 0-1 Ski Route and Lift Optimization

## At a glance
The team identifies smooth terrain nodes, clusters peaks and solves a 0-1 program for roughly 20 routes and five lifts. An AHP ranks Wasatch Peaks Ranch against existing resorts using size, vertical drop, trail variety, convenience and quality; all comparison matrices pass their consistency checks. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They reduce the terrain to candidate nodes and binary route decisions, minimize total slope distance while meeting the trail and difficulty requirements, and then treat ranking as a separate multi-criteria decision. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Normal vectors identify smooth nodes; cluster analysis supplies representative peaks and bowls. Binary variables select routes/lifts; normalized criteria and AHP weights generate the resort ranking (pp. 8–25).

## Data
The paper uses topographic map, resort comparison data and web elevation data. The source is the Wasatch topographic map or USGS/GIS elevation data, together with a resort comparison table and web references. Candidate paths, slope classes and skier/lift scenarios are derived rather than observed. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes the topographic data represent buildable terrain, slope grade is the main difficulty signal, and candidate paths can be filtered for rivers, outparcels and unsafe grades. Ranking weights represent skier or Olympic priorities, while lift simulations use stylized skier mixes and capacities (pp. 4–10). Its central choice is 13×13 binary node matrix, supported by cluster analysis of peaks; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
The paper checks feasibility, total distance, difficulty distribution and AHP consistency ratio. Alternative criteria weights and data processing are shown in tables (pp. 16–26).

## What makes it stand out
The compact binary formulation makes a large terrain tractable and gives a clear optimization objective. AHP’s consistency check demonstrates care in the ranking stage.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A contents page, letter, route appendix, AHP methodology and criteria tables make the paper easy to reproduce (pp. 2–4, 16–28).

## Notes for reuse
- A coarse 13×13 grid and hand-selected nodes may miss good routes. An imitator should use higher-resolution terrain and test sensitivity to AHP weights.
