# 2016B-6475: Vector Delivery Model for Warehouse Cover

## At a glance
The paper converts one-day UPS reachability into a state graph and first minimizes warehouse count, finding 26 locations with duplicated coverage in California and Texas. Vector diagrams and 0-1 programming then incorporate state tax and clothing-tax scenarios, with manual refinement of the final map. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They simplify each state into reachable/unreachable nodes, state the coverage objective, then add tax only after a minimum-warehouse baseline is known. A graph representation connects each candidate warehouse to states it can serve in one day. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
A binary matrix records one-day reachability. Greedy selection supplies a fast cover; 0-1 programming checks candidate sets. Vector maps show coverage and allow tax-weighted adjustments for parts II and III (pp. 4–22).

## Data
The paper uses UPS one-day map, state sales-tax table and ZIP/state geography. The source is the UPS ground transit map or extracted ZIP/pixel reachability, combined with state sales-tax and clothing-tax tables, population and road-access information. The unit of coverage is a stated modeling choice. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes UPS one-day reachability can be represented by states, ZIP codes or map pixels and that candidate warehouses cover every unit assigned to them. Taxes are applied at warehouse-state level, with clothing treated as a second tax schedule. Population and uninhabited areas are handled differently by each team and are reported as modeling choices (pp. 4–12). Its central choice is 27-by-27 coverage matrix, supported by greedy set cover; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Alternative covers are compared by warehouse count, reachability and tax. The paper tests whether adding clothing changes the selected states and discusses unpopulated areas and route assumptions (pp. 20–27).

## What makes it stand out
The matrix-plus-vector presentation makes a set-cover problem accessible and gives the reader both a computational answer and a map. Reporting alternative plans is useful when tax and coverage conflict.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A business letter states the 26-location baseline and alternatives before the technical paper. Diagrams, state tables and code appendices support reproducibility (pp. 1–3, 8–27).

## Notes for reuse
- The state-level abstraction ignores within-state population and highway variation. An imitator should distinguish area from population coverage and document how tax weights alter the objective.
