# 2016B-7057: ZIP-Center Warehouse Plan

## At a glance
The paper chooses central ZIP codes in each state, filters them by proximity to major roads, removes three warehouses serving only uninhabited areas, and recommends 25 locations. A state metric ranks sales and apparel tax liability, and warehouses are moved only when coverage is preserved. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They distinguish geographic coverage from inhabited coverage and treat the latter as the practical requirement. Candidate generation is deliberately simple and explainable, then tax is applied as a secondary criterion. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Task-by-task approach
| Official requirement | Their approach | Key output | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate locations | central ZIPs filtered by major-road proximity | 28 candidates then 25 inhabited-area sites | pp. 4–14 |

## Models and methods
State ZIP ranges generate central candidates; road proximity identifies feasible cities. A metric combines population and tax rate to rank state liability. Coverage maps and location tables compare 28, 25 and staged construction plans (pp. 4–19).

## Data
The paper uses UPS ZIP ranges, road maps, state populations and tax tables. The source is the UPS ground transit map or extracted ZIP/pixel reachability, combined with state sales-tax and clothing-tax tables, population and road-access information. The unit of coverage is a stated modeling choice. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes UPS one-day reachability can be represented by states, ZIP codes or map pixels and that candidate warehouses cover every unit assigned to them. Taxes are applied at warehouse-state level, with clothing treated as a second tax schedule. Population and uninhabited areas are handled differently by each team and are reported as modeling choices (pp. 4–12). Its central choice is central ZIP candidate selection, supported by roadway proximity filter; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
The team checks that the reduced 25-site plan covers inhabited areas and compares tax scenarios with and without clothing. It discusses phased construction as an implementation sensitivity (pp. 19–24).

## What makes it stand out
Removing uninhabited coverage is a clear, decision-relevant refinement. The staged build plan turns an abstract optimum into an investment roadmap.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A concise letter, map figures, metric definition and final site table make this one of the most usable papers for a nontechnical president (pp. 1–4, 16–24).

## Notes for reuse
- Central ZIP codes are proxies for demand and road access. An imitator should use actual population-weighted delivery points and confirm one-day service rather than infer it from state coverage.
