# 2017A-8393: Hexagon-Packed Drone Cluster Show

## At a glance
The team places about 400 drones for a Ferris wheel, dragon and solar-system display. Specific courses and positions, five-foot safety spacing and hexagonal ground packing produce a safe launch plan. Costs are estimated for purchase versus rental; display timing and FAA airspace are included. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They sketch each figure, allocate a finite drone budget, then check safety and logistics. The solar system uses colored spherical clusters, while Ferris wheel rotation and dragon motion provide animation. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Circles, spokes and spheres define point sets. Hexagonal packing minimizes ground area; vertical planes separate Ferris wheel spokes. Rotation and wave-like paths animate the first two displays, and orbital positions represent planets (pp. 4–23).

## Data
The paper uses display sketches, Intel examples, FAA and drone-cost references. The figures are team-designed point sets or traced display contours, supplemented by drone spacing, battery, safety, FAA and cost references. Coordinates and sampling resolution are generated within the model. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes a drone can hold a prescribed 3-D coordinate and follow a planned path, with a fixed safety separation and battery duration. Display point spacing, altitude, audience buffer and launch packing are explicit design choices; regulatory constraints are discussed as requirements to verify (pp. 4–10). Its central choice is explicit drone point placement, supported by hexagonal packing; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Drone counts, spacing, launch area, airspace, duration and costs are recomputed for each display. Sensitivity checks vary safety distance and display allocation (pp. 23–29).

## What makes it stand out
The paper treats operational questions—launch packing, rental/purchase cost and audience buffer—as part of the model rather than appendices. The solar-system choice is memorable and quantitatively specified.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A mayoral letter gives the 400-drone recommendation and cost options. Diagrams, a schedule and a dedicated safety/legality section support decision review (pp. 1–4, 18–29).

## Notes for reuse
- Cost estimates are approximate and FAA constraints may change. An imitator should distinguish geometric safety spacing from regulatory exclusion zones and verify battery endurance.
