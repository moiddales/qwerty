# 2016B-7211: Genetic Warehouse Coverage and Tax Trade-offs

## At a glance
A genetic algorithm searches warehouse ZIP-code sets. Thirty-two warehouses cover all mapped land, while 23 cover 95.89% of land and 99.6% of population. Tax-aware runs favor low-tax ZIP codes but reduce coverage, so the paper presents both options and a formula for choosing between them. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They explicitly separate land coverage, population coverage, tax and clothing. The president can select a point on the trade-off rather than receiving a false single optimum. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Candidate ZIP codes are chromosomes; fitness combines coverage and tax. Genetic operators evolve sets of fixed size, and a second objective includes clothing-tax exemptions. Result tables list ZIP codes and coverage/tax for multiple warehouse counts (pp. 4–35).

## Data
The paper uses UPS transit maps, ZIP data, population and state/clothing taxes. The source is the UPS ground transit map or extracted ZIP/pixel reachability, combined with state sales-tax and clothing-tax tables, population and road-access information. The unit of coverage is a stated modeling choice. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes UPS one-day reachability can be represented by states, ZIP codes or map pixels and that candidate warehouses cover every unit assigned to them. Taxes are applied at warehouse-state level, with clothing treated as a second tax schedule. Population and uninhabited areas are handled differently by each team and are reported as modeling choices (pp. 4–12). Its central choice is genetic algorithm set cover, supported by population-weighted coverage; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Runs compare 32, 23 and tax-focused sets; coverage and average tax are checked against UPS maps. Sensitivity to objective weighting and clothing-tax ranges is discussed (pp. 30–53).

## What makes it stand out
Presenting a Pareto-like menu is the paper’s strength: it admits that 100% land coverage is inefficient and quantifies the cost of saving tax.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A president’s letter leads with ZIP lists and headline percentages; technical sections explain the chromosome encoding, then appendices document code and runs (pp. 1–3, 30–65).

## Notes for reuse
- Genetic search quality depends on encoding and fitness weights. An imitator should seed or compare against greedy/exact solutions and report run-to-run variability.
