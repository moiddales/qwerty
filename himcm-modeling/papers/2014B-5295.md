# 2014B-5295: Five-Compartment Ebola Scenario Model

## At a glance
This paper extends SIR to Susceptible, Infected, Contagious, Removed and Deceased states. A one-week incubation separates infected from contagious people, and mortality, contact and infection probabilities drive weekly updates. A modified model treats children and elderly separately and tests doctors and prevention. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
The team treats the Indonesian village as a small closed population and then asks how the result changes when age risk, contacts and medical staffing are known. It answers the requested classification, recommendations, questions and revised model with the same state variables. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Five difference equations update each compartment. Parameters include infection given contact, death given contagiousness, and weekly contacts. Scenarios vary mortality, contact frequency, doctor count and prevention. Tables and graphs report deaths, peak infection and extinction time (pp. 4–12).

## Data
The paper uses prompt village counts, Ebola references and assumed parameters. The input is the village case supplied by the prompt. Teams supplement it with Ebola or disease-transmission references, assumed transition probabilities, GDP/distance data, vaccine efficacy or sentencing-style resource proxies as appropriate. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes a closed or nearly closed village during one modeled run, homogeneous contacts unless age groups are introduced, and fixed or sampled transition probabilities. Latent infection, contagiousness, mortality and intervention effects are explicit states or parameters. The teams state that sparse field data make these assumptions provisional (pp. 5–9). Its central choice is SIR-derived five-state difference equations, supported by age-stratified mortality; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Original and modified scenarios are compared across parameter choices; the paper includes sensitivity/error analysis and recommendations (pp. 7–12). It checks whether intervention changes the epidemic trajectory and acknowledges that constant probabilities are a strong simplification.

## What makes it stand out
The explicit contagious sub-state makes the incubation assumption operational and keeps deaths visible rather than hiding them in a generic removed group. The age-stratified revision responds directly to the new evidence.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
An article-style paper includes a variable index, model-design table, graphs, and a news letter (pp. 1–3, 5–14).

## Notes for reuse
- The model’s weekly time step and constant probabilities can distort fast outbreaks. An imitator should use observed serial intervals, separate isolation from recovery, and report uncertainty around mortality.
