# 2016B-6829: Approximation Algorithm for Shop and Ship

## At a glance
The team identifies a set-cover structure, extracts UPS map pixels and ZIP-code reachability, and replaces infeasible brute force with a greedy approximation. It reports 23 warehouses for 95.89% land and 99.6% population coverage, and a tax-adjusted 22-warehouse plan covering 95.26% of land. Clothing-tax changes do not alter the recommendation in their tested range. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They make the computational bottleneck explicit: map data extraction and NP-hard cover search. The approximation chooses the candidate with maximum incremental coverage, then modifies that gain with tax. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
A crawler extracts map pixels; a binary matrix links ZIP/candidate warehouses to one-day coverage. Greedy set cover runs in near-quadratic time; a tax-adjusted score trades marginal area against state tax. Complexity and failed brute-force attempts are documented (pp. 8–28).

## Data
The paper uses UPS transit maps, ZIP codes, state tax tables and clothing tax. The source is the UPS ground transit map or extracted ZIP/pixel reachability, combined with state sales-tax and clothing-tax tables, population and road-access information. The unit of coverage is a stated modeling choice. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes UPS one-day reachability can be represented by states, ZIP codes or map pixels and that candidate warehouses cover every unit assigned to them. Taxes are applied at warehouse-state level, with clothing treated as a second tax schedule. Population and uninhabited areas are handled differently by each team and are reported as modeling choices (pp. 4–12). Its central choice is web crawler and pixel extraction, supported by set-cover approximation; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Coverage is checked against the extracted map and alternative warehouse counts. The team compares 100% coverage with lower-count plans and tests clothing-tax perturbations (pp. 27–29).

## What makes it stand out
The paper teaches a good modeling move: recognize NP-hard structure, measure the cost of exact search, then justify approximation with complexity and quality metrics. The extraction pipeline is unusually transparent.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
A president’s letter gives the decision first; a contents page, failed-algorithm section, complexity table and appendices let readers audit the pipeline (pp. 1–3, 13–30).

## Notes for reuse
- Pixel extraction and land coverage may not equal population or delivery time. An imitator should validate map parsing and report approximation gaps against small exact instances.
