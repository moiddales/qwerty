# 2017B-8201: GIS Fall Lines, Solar Aspect and Lift Queues

## At a glance
The team combines USGS topography, slope gradient, fall lines and solar aspect to locate safe trails, then ranks the proposed resort against North American sites. A NetLogo agent model evaluates lift queues and skier flow; watershed analysis checks path stability. (pp. 1–3).

## How they read the problem
They prioritize ridges and fall lines, exclude rivers/outparcels, classify grade into beginner/intermediate/difficult bands, and use solar exposure to avoid melt/refreeze. Resort ranking and queue simulation are downstream checks. They keep the official deliverable in view and use stated assumptions to make the decision variables finite (pp. 3–6).

## Models and methods
Raster gradients and vector fields generate candidate fall lines; image masks classify grades and filter terrain. A multi-criteria score ranks resorts. NetLogo agents move skiers through lifts and trails, recording wait times (pp. 7–19).

## Data
The paper uses USGS DEM/topographic data, resort comparison table and GIS maps. The source is the Wasatch topographic map or USGS/GIS elevation data, together with a resort comparison table and web references. Candidate paths, slope classes and skier/lift scenarios are derived rather than observed. It distinguishes prompt values, cited constants and generated scenarios so that a reader can see which inputs would need recalibration for a new setting (pp. 4–8).

## Assumptions
This team assumes the topographic data represent buildable terrain, slope grade is the main difficulty signal, and candidate paths can be filtered for rivers, outparcels and unsafe grades. Ranking weights represent skier or Olympic priorities, while lift simulations use stylized skier mixes and capacities (pp. 4–10). Its central choice is gradient vector fields, supported by fall-line extraction; those choices determine which behavior is visible in the reported results (pp. 4–8).
## Validation, sensitivity and robustness
Sensitivity varies number of lifts and watershed thresholds. Overlay comparisons between Mathematica vector fields and SURFER watershed paths test geometric robustness (pp. 18–24).

## What makes it stand out
The paper integrates environmental safety, geometry and operations. Cross-checking two path-generation methods is a valuable robustness habit.

## Writing and presentation worth learning
Maps dominate the explanation, with a professional letter, rating table, lift graphs and code appendix (pp. 1–4, 13–24).

## Notes for reuse
- Fall-line emphasis excludes trails that could be engineered across contours, and edge raster errors are acknowledged. An imitator should model grooming, construction and lift capacity explicitly.
